📖 The Story — Building Amazon Without Understanding Amazon
Why Event Storming Exists

Your manager says "Build Amazon." You immediately create Orders, Products, Payments tables. Weeks later: "How do returns work?" "How do gift cards work?" "How do sellers manage inventory?" Every sprint becomes database rewrites. The problem wasn't bad coding—it was poor business understanding. Before designing software, we must understand how the business behaves. This is why Event Storming was created.

🎯 What is Event Storming?
Event Storming: A collaborative workshop used to discover how a business works by identifying events. Instead of asking "What tables do we need?" we ask "What actually happens?" Business comes first. Software comes later.

📋 Event Storming Elements

🟧 Events (Orange)

Something that happened. Past tense. Immutable facts.

🟦 Commands (Blue)

User intent. Requests to do something. Imperative.

🟨 Aggregates (Yellow)

Business logic boundaries. Enforce rules. Protect invariants.

🟪 Policies (Purple)

Automated reactions to events. "When X happens, do Y."

🟩 Read Models (Green)

Optimized queries. Dashboards. User interfaces.

🟥 External Systems (Red)

Third-party integrations. Payment gateways. Email services.

🔄 Complete Flow
Command: Request Ride ↓ Aggregate: Ride (Validates, enforces rules) ↓ Event: RideRequested ↓ Policy: Match Driver (Automated reaction) ↓ Event: DriverMatched ↓ Read Model: Driver Dashboard (Optimized query)
🏭 Production Example
Real World

Amazon Event Storming Workshop

Amazon conducts Event Storming workshops with engineers, product managers, and domain experts. They identify 200+ events: CustomerRegistered, CartCreated, OrderCreated, PaymentCompleted, InventoryReserved, ShipmentCreated, Delivered, ReviewRequested. These events become the backbone of their microservice architecture. Each cluster of related events becomes a bounded context, which eventually becomes a microservice.

🎯 Interview Callout
Q
"Why perform Event Storming before designing microservices?"
"Event Storming identifies business workflows, domain boundaries, and ownership before implementation. It helps teams discover natural service boundaries and prevents designing microservices around technical layers rather than business capabilities."
🧠 Memory Trick
Business → Events → Commands → Aggregates → Policies → Read Models → Microservices. Always start with business, not databases.
