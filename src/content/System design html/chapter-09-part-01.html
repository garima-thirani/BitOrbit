📖 The Story — The Startup That Became Too Successful
Day 1 vs Year 3

5 engineers, 500 users, one database → 400 engineers, 50M users, still one database.

Day 1: 5 engineers, 500 users, one product, one database. Deployment takes 30 seconds. Every engineer understands the entire codebase. Life is good. Year 3: 400 engineers, 80 product teams, 50M users. The architecture is still one giant application. A tiny change in Profile Page requires deploying the entire application. A bug in Recommendation Engine crashes Checkout. An Analytics query locks the Orders table. Customers cannot purchase products. Engineers begin saying "Please don't deploy today." The problem isn't bad code. The architecture no longer matches the organization.

🎯 Conway's Law
Conway's Law: "Organizations design systems that mirror their communication structure." Independent teams naturally build independent services. One giant team builds one giant application.

🏭 Production Examples
Real World

Amazon, Netflix, Uber

Amazon: Started with monolith, evolved to 1000+ microservices over years using Strangler Fig Pattern.

Netflix: Moved from monolith to microservices to handle 200M+ users with independent scaling.

Uber: Started with monolithic Rails app, migrated to microservices as team grew from 10 to 1000+ engineers.

🎯 Interview Callout
Q
"Why did the industry move from monoliths to microservices?"
"As engineering teams and user bases grew, monoliths became difficult to deploy, scale, and maintain. Microservices allow independent deployment, independent scaling, better team ownership, and improved fault isolation, making them better suited for large organizations."
🧠 Memory Trick
Start Monolith → Grow Team → Extract Services → Microservices. Architecture evolves with organization.
