📖 The Story — The Two Data Centers
Network Partition Problem

Amazon has two data centers. The network cable between them fails. Now what?

Imagine Amazon has two data centers: US-East and US-West. Normally, they communicate continuously. Suddenly, the network cable between them fails. A customer places an order in US-East. Another customer tries to read the same data from US-West. Question: Should US-West return old data? Or refuse the request? This is the CAP problem. During a network partition, you must choose between Consistency and Availability.

🎯 What is CAP?
CAP Theorem: When a network partition occurs in a distributed system, it is impossible to simultaneously guarantee both strong consistency and availability. Since network partitions are unavoidable, systems must choose between Consistency and Availability during failures.

✅ Why CA Doesn't Exist
Why CA is impossible: Network failures cannot be prevented. Cables break, routers fail, clouds have outages. If two nodes cannot communicate, Partition Tolerance becomes mandatory. Therefore, the real choice during a partition is CP (Consistency + Partition Tolerance) or AP (Availability + Partition Tolerance).
🏭 Production Examples
Real World

Google Spanner vs Apache Cassandra

Google Spanner: Prioritizes Consistency. Uses TrueTime clocks for global transactions. CP system. Perfect for financial data where correctness is critical.

Apache Cassandra: Prioritizes Availability. Always responds, even during partitions. AP system. Perfect for analytics and social media where temporary inconsistency is acceptable.

🎯 Interview Callout
Q
"Is CAP theorem about choosing 2 out of 3?"
"No, that's a common misconception. Partition tolerance is mandatory in distributed systems because network failures are inevitable. The real trade-off is between Consistency and Availability during a partition. When the network works normally, many systems provide all three. CAP only matters during failures."
