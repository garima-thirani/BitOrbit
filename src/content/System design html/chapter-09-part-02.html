📖 The Story — The "User Service" Disaster
One Service to Rule Them All

Engineers build "User Service" containing profiles, payments, wishlists, addresses, orders, and loyalty points. After 3 years: 2M lines of code, 60 engineers, hundreds of APIs. Every team modifies the same service. Nobody knows who owns what. The "User Service" has become another monolith.

The problem wasn't Microservices. The problem was poor boundaries. Microservices are not defined by number of APIs or databases. They are defined by business responsibilities. Good boundaries reduce coupling. Bad boundaries recreate monoliths. This is why Domain-Driven Design (DDD) exists—to help you find natural service boundaries based on business capabilities, not technical convenience.

🎯 What is Domain-Driven Design?
Domain-Driven Design (DDD): A software design approach introduced by Eric Evans that focuses on modeling software around the business domain instead of technical layers. Instead of thinking "Controllers → Services → Repositories", DDD thinks "Orders → Payments → Inventory → Shipping". Business first, technology second.

📋 Ubiquitous Language
Ubiquitous Language: Everyone in the organization uses the same terminology. If Business says "Order", Engineering says "Order", Database says "Order", Support says "Order". No more "Customer vs User vs Client vs Member" confusion. This single shared vocabulary eliminates misunderstandings and ensures everyone talks about the same thing.

🔄 Aggregates & Aggregate Roots
Aggregate: A cluster of related entities treated as a single unit. The Aggregate Root is the only entry point. Example: Order Aggregate contains Order, Order Items, Shipping Address, Payment Status. No service should directly modify Order Items—everything goes through the Order Aggregate Root. This enforces business rules and maintains consistency.
✅ Anti-Corruption Layer (ACL)
Anti-Corruption Layer: A translation layer between your modern service and a legacy system. If Legacy returns "Cust_ID" but your service expects "CustomerId", the ACL translates between them. This prevents legacy concepts from polluting your clean domain model. Think of it as a diplomatic translator between two countries speaking different languages.
🏭 Production Example
Real World

Amazon's Service Boundaries

Amazon doesn't have a "Shopping Service" containing everything. Instead, they have separate services: Catalog (product info), Cart (shopping cart), Orders (purchase workflow), Payments (money), Inventory (stock), Shipping (delivery), Recommendations (suggestions). Each service owns its business capability, its data, and its deployment. This allows 1000+ services to evolve independently without blocking each other.

🎯 Interview Callout
Q
"How do you decide Microservice boundaries?"
"Microservice boundaries should follow business capabilities rather than technical layers. A good candidate service has distinct business rules, independent ownership, independent scaling requirements, and its own lifecycle. Use DDD to identify bounded contexts, then extract services around those boundaries."
🧠 Memory Trick
Business Capability → Bounded Context → Aggregate → Service. Design services around what the business does, not how the code is organized.
