# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why does DynamoDB automatically split partitions?

### Ideal Answer

DynamoDB automatically splits partitions when storage size or request throughput exceeds internal limits. This distributes load across additional storage nodes while maintaining application transparency.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why does Cassandra use virtual nodes?

### Ideal Answer

Virtual nodes improve load balancing, simplify node addition and removal, reduce data movement during rebalancing, and allow heterogeneous hardware to participate efficiently in the cluster.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why does Uber use geographic sharding?

### Ideal Answer

Most ride requests occur within a local geographic area. Geo-sharding keeps drivers, riders, and trip data close together, reducing latency and minimizing cross-region communication.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why is tenant-based sharding suitable for Shopify?

### Ideal Answer

Each merchant primarily accesses its own data. Tenant-based sharding isolates workloads, simplifies backups and migrations, and allows large merchants to be moved to dedicated infrastructure when necessary.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the sharding architecture for a global e-commerce platform serving hundreds of millions of users.

### Ideal Answer

Partition customer data using a stable shard key such as CustomerID with hash-based routing for even distribution. Geo-shard data across major regions to reduce latency, replicate critical information across regions for resilience, use consistent hashing with virtual nodes for easy scaling, monitor hotspots continuously, perform online rebalancing, maintain read replicas per shard, and automate capacity planning and failover to ensure high availability and low latency worldwide.

# Revision Notes

- Production sharding strategies differ according to workload and business requirements.
- Google Spanner uses range partitioning with globally replicated tablets.
- DynamoDB relies on hash partitioning and automatic partition splitting.
- Cassandra combines consistent hashing with virtual nodes for balanced scaling.
- CockroachDB provides distributed SQL using range-based partitioning and Raft consensus.
- Uber reduces latency through geographic sharding.
- Shopify isolates tenants to simplify operations and scalability.
- Automation, monitoring, and online rebalancing are essential for operating large distributed database clusters.
