# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why are cross-shard joins expensive?

### Ideal Answer

Cross-shard joins require data to be transferred across the network, increasing latency, serialization overhead, and temporary storage requirements. Whenever possible, related data should be co-located to avoid distributed joins.

---

## Question 2

### Question

How does Two-Phase Commit ensure atomicity?

### Ideal Answer

The coordinator first asks every participant to prepare. If all participants acknowledge readiness, the coordinator instructs them to commit. If any participant fails during the prepare phase, all participants roll back, ensuring atomicity across shards.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why do modern microservices often prefer the Saga pattern?

### Ideal Answer

The Saga pattern replaces one large distributed transaction with a sequence of local transactions coordinated through events or orchestration. It avoids long-held locks, scales better, and is more resilient, though it provides eventual rather than immediate consistency.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What is idempotency, and why is it important?

### Ideal Answer

Idempotency ensures that repeating the same request produces the same outcome without unintended side effects. It is essential in distributed systems because retries caused by network failures or timeouts are common.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the transaction architecture for a global payment platform where users and merchants are stored on different shards.

### Ideal Answer

Keep most operations local by co-locating related entities where possible. Use Saga workflows for long-running business processes such as payment authorization, inventory reservation, and notifications. Reserve Two-Phase Commit only for operations requiring strict atomicity. Implement idempotency keys, event-driven communication, compensation actions, retry mechanisms, monitoring, and distributed tracing to ensure reliability at scale.

# Revision Notes

- Cross-shard queries commonly use the Scatter-Gather pattern.
- Distributed joins are expensive because they require network communication.
- Two-Phase Commit provides strong atomicity but introduces blocking.
- Three-Phase Commit reduces blocking but is rarely adopted in practice.
- The Saga pattern decomposes distributed workflows into local transactions with compensating actions.
- Idempotency is essential for handling retries safely.
- Large-scale distributed systems minimize cross-shard transactions through careful data placement and event-driven architectures.
