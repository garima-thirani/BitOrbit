# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why is rebalancing necessary in a sharded database?

### Ideal Answer

As workloads evolve, data and traffic become unevenly distributed across shards. Rebalancing redistributes ownership to prevent hotspots, improve resource utilization, and maintain predictable performance.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the difference between a hot key and a hot partition?

### Ideal Answer

A hot key is a single record receiving unusually high traffic, while a hot partition is an entire shard or partition experiencing excessive load due to many popular records or poor data distribution.

---

## Question 3

### Question

How does online resharding avoid downtime?

### Ideal Answer

Online resharding copies data incrementally, synchronizes ongoing updates through dual writes, validates consistency, and performs a controlled cutover so that users continue accessing the system throughout the migration.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why are virtual nodes useful during rebalancing?

### Ideal Answer

Virtual nodes allow only small portions of data ownership to move between servers instead of relocating entire shards. This results in better load balancing and significantly less data movement.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Your database cluster has 100 shards. One shard receives ten times more traffic than the others. How would you fix it?

### Ideal Answer

First identify whether the issue is caused by a hot key or a hot partition. For hot keys, introduce caching, request throttling, counter sharding, or asynchronous processing. For hot partitions, rebalance virtual nodes, split overloaded shards, migrate data online, update routing metadata, and continuously monitor traffic to verify balanced load after migration.

# Revision Notes

- Rebalancing keeps storage and traffic evenly distributed across shards.
- Hot keys overload individual records, while hot partitions overload entire shards.
- Online resharding enables live data migration without downtime.
- Dual writes and validation ensure safe migrations.
- Virtual nodes reduce the amount of data moved during rebalancing.
- Split and merge operations adapt the cluster to changing workloads.
- Continuous monitoring is essential for detecting and correcting imbalance before it affects users.
