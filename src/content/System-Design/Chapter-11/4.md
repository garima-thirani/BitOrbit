# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why does Kafka use partitions?

### Ideal Answer

Partitions enable horizontal scalability and parallel processing. Each partition is an ordered append-only log, allowing multiple consumers to process different partitions simultaneously while preserving ordering within each partition.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is an offset in Kafka?

### Ideal Answer

An offset is the unique sequential position of a message within a partition. Consumers track offsets to know which messages have already been processed and where to resume after failures.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why does Kafka retain messages after they are consumed?

### Ideal Answer

Kafka stores events for a configurable retention period, allowing consumers to replay historical data, recover after failures, rebuild application state, and support multiple independent consumers without duplicating storage.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What is the purpose of consumer groups?

### Ideal Answer

Consumer groups allow multiple consumers to share partitions of a topic, enabling parallel processing while ensuring each message is processed only once within the group.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design a Kafka deployment for an e-commerce platform processing 20 million orders per day.

### Ideal Answer

Create dedicated topics such as `orders`, `payments`, and `shipments` with sufficient partitions to support expected consumer parallelism. Partition order events by `CustomerID` or `OrderID` to preserve ordering for related events. Configure a replication factor of at least three, use `ACK=all` for durability, monitor consumer lag and ISR health, enable retention for replay, and use log compaction where the latest entity state is sufficient.

# Revision Notes

- Kafka is a distributed append-only log rather than a traditional message queue.
- Topics are divided into partitions for scalability.
- Ordering is guaranteed only within a partition.
- Offsets uniquely identify message positions.
- Consumer groups enable parallel consumption.
- Replication and leader election provide fault tolerance.
