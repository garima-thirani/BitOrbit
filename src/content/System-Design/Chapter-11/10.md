# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why are distributed logs append-only?

### Ideal Answer

Append-only logs preserve the complete history of events, simplify replication, improve write throughput, and enable replay for auditing, recovery, and analytics.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the purpose of a Schema Registry?

### Ideal Answer

A Schema Registry stores message schemas centrally, ensuring producers and consumers agree on message formats while enabling compatibility checks and safe schema evolution.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why are Avro and Protobuf preferred over JSON in production messaging systems?

### Ideal Answer

Avro and Protobuf provide compact binary serialization, strong typing, schema validation, and compatibility support, reducing bandwidth and improving processing performance compared to JSON.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why should retry logic use exponential backoff with jitter?

### Ideal Answer

Exponential backoff prevents immediate repeated failures from overwhelming downstream systems, while jitter randomizes retry timing to avoid synchronized retry storms across many consumers.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design a resilient event processing pipeline for a payment platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use Kafka as the distributed log with Avro schemas managed through a Schema Registry. Configure consumers with exponential backoff and jitter for transient failures, move permanently failing events to a Dead Letter Topic after a configurable retry limit, monitor consumer lag and DLQ size, and enable replay after bugs are fixed. Ensure schemas evolve using backward-compatible changes and maintain immutable event history for auditing.

# Revision Notes

- Distributed logs provide immutable, append-only event storage.
- Log retention preserves history, while log compaction preserves the latest value per key.
- Schema Registries manage message contracts across producers and consumers.
- Avro and Protobuf provide efficient binary serialization and strong compatibility.
- Schema evolution must preserve backward and forward compatibility whenever possible.
- Exponential backoff with jitter prevents retry storms.
- Dead Letter Queues isolate permanently failing messages for later investigation and replay.
