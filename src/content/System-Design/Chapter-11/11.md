# Case Study 1 — LinkedIn: Building Apache Kafka

## Business Problem

By 2010,

LinkedIn had

hundreds of services.

Every service needed

activity events.

Examples

- User updates profile
- New connection
- Job application
- Feed generation
- Search indexing
- Notifications

Originally,

services communicated

using

point-to-point messaging.

```text
Service A

↓

Service B

↓

Service C
```

Problems

- Tight coupling
- Difficult scaling
- Event duplication
- Poor replay support

---

## Solution

LinkedIn

created

Apache Kafka.

Architecture

```text
Services

↓

Kafka Topics

↓

Consumers
```

---

## Event Flow

User creates post.

```text
User

↓

Post Service

↓

Kafka Topic

↓

Feed Service

↓

Search

↓

Notifications

↓

Analytics

↓

Recommendations
```

One event.

Many consumers.

---

## Why Kafka?

Reasons

✔ Replay

✔ High throughput

✔ Horizontal scaling

✔ Durable storage

✔ Event streaming

---

## Scaling Strategy

- Hundreds of brokers
- Thousands of topics
- Millions of events/sec
- Consumer Groups
- Partitioning

---

## Failure Recovery

Broker fails.

Replica

becomes leader.

Consumers

continue processing.

No downtime.

---

## Lessons

Kafka

was built

because

existing queues

could not

scale

to LinkedIn's needs.

---

# Interview Takeaway

When asked

"Why Kafka?"

mention

Replay,

Durability,

Distributed Log,

Horizontal Scaling,

Consumer Groups.

# Final Interview Questions

## Question 1

### Question

When should you choose Kafka instead of RabbitMQ?

### Ideal Answer

Choose Kafka when you need durable event streaming, high throughput, replay capability, long-term event storage, and analytics pipelines. Choose RabbitMQ for reliable task distribution, intelligent routing, RPC, and workflow processing.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why do financial systems rely heavily on idempotency?

### Ideal Answer

Financial systems cannot tolerate duplicate business operations such as charging a customer twice. Idempotency ensures repeated processing of the same event produces the same final result, making retries safe.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why is Event Replay valuable?

### Ideal Answer

Replay enables systems to recover after failures, rebuild state, create new projections, support analytics, audit historical activity, and onboard new consumers without affecting producers.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why are Consumer Groups important?

### Ideal Answer

Consumer Groups allow multiple consumers to process partitions of the same topic in parallel, increasing throughput while ensuring each message is processed only once within the group.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the messaging infrastructure for a global e-commerce platform serving hundreds of millions of users.

### Ideal Answer

Use Kafka as the central event backbone with partitioned topics for orders, payments, inventory, and shipping. Publish immutable domain events, process them through independent consumer groups, use Apache Flink for real-time analytics and fraud detection, maintain schemas with a Schema Registry, implement retries with exponential backoff and Dead Letter Topics, enforce idempotency for business-critical operations, enable event replay through durable retention, monitor consumer lag and broker health, and autoscale consumers independently based on traffic.
