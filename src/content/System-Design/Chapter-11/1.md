# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why are messaging systems used in distributed architectures?

### Ideal Answer

Messaging systems decouple services, allowing producers and consumers to operate independently. This improves scalability, resilience, fault isolation, and enables asynchronous processing without blocking upstream services.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the difference between a Queue and a Topic?

### Ideal Answer

A queue delivers each message to a single consumer, making it suitable for work distribution. A topic broadcasts each message to multiple subscribers, making it suitable for event-driven architectures.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is a Dead Letter Queue?

### Ideal Answer

A Dead Letter Queue stores messages that repeatedly fail processing. Instead of retrying indefinitely, problematic messages are isolated for manual investigation while allowing the rest of the system to continue operating.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why is idempotency important in messaging systems?

### Ideal Answer

Messages may be delivered more than once due to retries or failures. Idempotent consumers ensure that processing the same message multiple times produces the same result, preventing duplicate operations such as charging a customer twice.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the messaging architecture for an online shopping platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use an event-driven architecture with a message broker. The Order Service publishes an `OrderCreated` event to a topic. Inventory, Payment, Shipping, Analytics, Notification, and Fraud Detection services subscribe independently. Messages are persistent, consumers acknowledge successful processing, retries are managed automatically, failed messages are moved to a Dead Letter Queue, and idempotency keys prevent duplicate processing.

# Revision Notes

- Messaging enables asynchronous communication between distributed services.
- Producers and consumers communicate through brokers rather than directly.
- Queues distribute work to one consumer, while topics broadcast events to many consumers.
- ACKs, retries, and DLQs improve reliability.
- Idempotency is essential for safe retry behavior.
- Message ordering matters for stateful workflows.
- Events should describe completed actions, not future commands.
