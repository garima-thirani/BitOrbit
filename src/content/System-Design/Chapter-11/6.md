# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

What is the biggest architectural difference between Kafka and Pulsar?

### Ideal Answer

Kafka combines compute and storage within brokers, while Pulsar separates them. Brokers handle message routing and coordination, whereas Apache BookKeeper provides distributed persistent storage. This separation enables independent scaling and cloud-native deployments.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why does Pulsar use BookKeeper?

### Ideal Answer

BookKeeper manages durable, replicated storage independently of brokers. It improves scalability, fault tolerance, and recovery while allowing brokers to remain lightweight and focus on message routing.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What subscription models does Pulsar support?

### Ideal Answer

Pulsar supports Exclusive, Shared, Failover, and Key_Shared subscriptions. These models allow applications to choose between strict ordering, parallel processing, high availability, or ordering based on message keys.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why is Pulsar considered cloud-native?

### Ideal Answer

Pulsar separates compute from storage, supports built-in geo-replication, multi-tenancy, tiered storage, and lightweight brokers, making it well suited for elastic cloud deployments.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design a multi-tenant messaging platform for a SaaS provider.

### Ideal Answer

Deploy Pulsar brokers across multiple regions with Apache BookKeeper clusters for durable replicated storage. Isolate customers using tenants and namespaces, enable partitioned topics for scalability, configure Shared or Key_Shared subscriptions depending on ordering requirements, use tiered storage for historical data, enable geo-replication for disaster recovery, and monitor backlog, replication lag, storage usage, and broker health for autoscaling and operational visibility.

# Revision Notes

- Pulsar separates compute (brokers) from storage (BookKeeper).
- BookKeeper stores replicated ledgers containing ordered message entries.
- Multiple subscription types support different workload patterns.
- Namespaces provide multi-tenant isolation and policy management.
- Built-in geo-replication and tiered storage simplify global deployments.
- Pulsar Functions and Pulsar IO extend the platform with lightweight processing and integrations.
- Pulsar is well suited for cloud-native, multi-tenant messaging systems that combine event streaming with traditional queue semantics.
