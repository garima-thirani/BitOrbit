# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why do distributed systems use message queues?

### Ideal Answer

Message queues decouple producers from consumers, absorb traffic spikes, improve fault tolerance, enable asynchronous processing, and allow independent scaling of different system components.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the difference between FIFO and Standard queues?

### Ideal Answer

FIFO queues preserve message ordering and often prevent duplicates, making them suitable for financial or transactional workloads. Standard queues prioritize throughput and scalability, where ordering is less important.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is a Visibility Timeout?

### Ideal Answer

A visibility timeout temporarily hides a message after a consumer receives it. If the consumer successfully acknowledges the message, it is deleted. Otherwise, after the timeout expires, the message becomes available for another consumer to process.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why is queue depth an important metric?

### Ideal Answer

Queue depth indicates the number of pending messages waiting to be processed. A continuously growing queue suggests that producers are generating work faster than consumers can process it, signaling the need for scaling or troubleshooting.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design a background job processing system for an e-commerce platform during Black Friday.

### Ideal Answer

Place incoming jobs into durable message queues. Use competing consumers that scale horizontally based on queue depth. Configure appropriate visibility timeouts, implement acknowledgements, retries with exponential backoff, and Dead Letter Queues for permanently failing jobs. Continuously monitor queue depth, consumer lag, processing latency, and retry counts to trigger autoscaling and operational alerts.

# Revision Notes

- Queues buffer work between producers and consumers.
- FIFO queues preserve ordering, while Standard queues maximize throughput.
- Visibility timeouts enable reliable message redelivery after failures.
- Queue depth is a critical health metric.
