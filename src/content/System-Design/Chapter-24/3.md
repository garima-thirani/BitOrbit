# Introduction

One of the biggest differences between junior and senior engineers is **how they make decisions.**

Junior engineers often ask

> "Which database should I use?"

Senior engineers ask

> "What are my requirements, and which database best satisfies them?"

Similarly,

they don't ask

> "Should I use Kafka?"

Instead,

they ask

> "Do I need event streaming?"

Decision matrices help engineers evaluate technologies objectively rather than relying on personal preference.

---

# What is a Decision Matrix?

A Decision Matrix compares multiple solutions against a common set of requirements.

Instead of guessing,

you score technologies based on

- Performance
- Scalability
- Consistency
- Complexity
- Cost
- Operational Overhead

This makes architectural decisions repeatable and justifiable.

---

# Real-Life Analogy

Imagine buying a car.

You don't ask

> "Which car is the best?"

Instead, you compare

- Budget
- Fuel Efficiency
- Safety
- Seating Capacity
- Maintenance
- Performance

The "best" car depends on your needs.

System Design follows the same principle.

# CAP Decision Matrix

Earlier in this handbook, you learned the CAP Theorem.

When a network partition occurs,

a distributed system can prioritize

- Consistency (C)
- Availability (A)

but not both simultaneously.

The decision depends entirely on the application's requirements.

---

## CAP Decision Matrix

| Application | Consistency | Availability | Recommendation |
|--------------|-------------|--------------|----------------|
| Banking | High | Medium | CP |
| Stock Trading | High | Medium | CP |
| Payment Gateway | High | Medium | CP |
| Social Media Feed | Medium | High | AP |
| Messaging Timeline | Medium | High | AP |
| Product Catalog | Medium | High | AP |
| DNS | Low | Very High | AP |
| Recommendation System | Medium | High | AP |

---

## Decision Tree

```text
Need Strong Consistency?

        │
        ▼
      Yes
        │
        ▼
Choose CP

        │
        ▼
No

        │
        ▼
Need Maximum Availability?

        │
        ▼
Choose AP
```

---

## Interview Tip

Always explain

**why**

consistency or availability matters.

Do not simply say

"I'll choose Cassandra."

# Caching Decision Matrix

Caching improves performance,

but different caching strategies solve different problems.

---

## Comparison

| Strategy | Best For | Advantages | Drawbacks |
|----------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Cache Aside | Read-heavy systems | Simple | Cache Miss Penalty |
| Read Through | Managed Cache | Transparent | Slightly Complex |
| Write Through | Data Consistency | Fresh Cache | Slower Writes |
| Write Back | High Write Throughput | Fast Writes | Risk of Data Loss |
| Write Around | Large Write Workloads | Prevents Cache Pollution | Initial Cache Miss |

---

## Decision Tree

```text
Read Heavy?

        │
        ▼
      Yes

        │
        ▼
Cache Aside
```

```text
Need Strong Cache Consistency?

↓

Write Through
```

```text
Write Heavy?

↓

Write Back
```

---

## Real-Life Example

News Website

↓

Cache Aside

because

articles are read far more often than updated.

---

Payment Processing

↓

Write Through

because

cached balances must always be correct.

# Sharding Decision Matrix

When one database can no longer scale,

sharding becomes necessary.

The challenge is deciding

how to split the data.

---

## Comparison

| Strategy | Advantages | Disadvantages | Best For |
|----------|------------|---------------|-----------|
| Hash Sharding | Even Distribution | Poor Range Queries | User IDs |
| Range Sharding | Efficient Range Queries | Hotspots | Time-series Data |
| Directory Sharding | Flexible | Additional Lookup | Enterprise Systems |
| Geo Sharding | Low Latency | Operational Complexity | Global Applications |
| Composite Sharding | Highly Flexible | Most Complex | Very Large Systems |

---

## Decision Tree

```text
Need Equal Distribution?

↓

Hash Sharding
```

```text
Need Range Queries?

↓

Range Sharding
```

```text
Global Users?

↓

Geo Sharding
```

---

## Real-Life Example

Instagram

↓

Hash User ID

because

users are evenly distributed.

---

Uber

↓

Geo Sharding

because

drivers and riders are location dependent.

# Interview Tips

Interviewers rarely expect a single "correct" answer.

Instead, they evaluate whether you can justify your decisions.

When choosing any technology,

always explain

- Why it fits the requirements.
- What trade-offs it introduces.
- What alternatives you considered.
- Why you rejected those alternatives.

This demonstrates architectural maturity.

# Key Takeaways

- Decision matrices help engineers choose technologies systematically rather than relying on intuition.
- CAP decisions depend on whether consistency or availability is more important for the application.
- Cache strategies should be selected based on read/write patterns and consistency requirements.
- Sharding strategies depend on access patterns, data distribution, and query requirements.
- Database selection should be driven by data models, scalability, consistency, and workload characteristics.
- Storage systems should be chosen according to the type of data being stored and accessed.
- Messaging technologies differ significantly in throughput, ordering, and delivery guarantees.
- AI systems introduce new architectural decisions such as RAG, Vector Databases, Model Routing, and Semantic Caching.
- The best architects explain **why** a technology is chosen and what trade-offs it introduces.
