# Most Common Interview Mistakes

Avoid these pitfalls:

- Starting without gathering requirements.
- Ignoring capacity estimation.
- Overengineering simple systems.
- Forgetting trade-offs.
- Ignoring failures and disaster recovery.
- Forgetting security and observability.
- Choosing technologies because they are popular instead of appropriate.
- Not communicating your reasoning.

# Final Mental Model

Whenever you encounter a new system design problem, think:

```text
What am I building?

↓

How big will it become?

↓

Where will it fail?

↓

How can I scale it?

↓

How can I make it reliable?

↓

How will I monitor it?

↓

How will I secure it?

↓

Can I explain every decision?
```

---

# Final Takeaways

- Great system designers think from **requirements to architecture**, not from technologies to architecture.
- Every technology exists to solve a specific problem.
- Scalability, reliability, security, and observability should be considered from the beginning.
- Production systems evolve incrementally—start simple and optimize based on measured bottlenecks.
- Clear communication and well-reasoned trade-offs are often more valuable than choosing the "perfect" technology.
