# Engineering Notes

## Reads Scale More Easily Than Writes

Leader–Follower

Adding replicas

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why is Leader–Follower replication widely used?

### Ideal Answer

Leader–Follower replication simplifies write coordination by allowing only one server to accept writes while followers replicate changes. It provides high availability, read scalability, and ordered writes, making it suitable for many production systems.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the difference between synchronous and asynchronous replication?

### Ideal Answer

Synchronous replication waits for replica acknowledgments before confirming writes, providing strong consistency but higher latency. Asynchronous replication confirms writes immediately and updates replicas later, improving performance at the cost of temporary inconsistency.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is replication lag?

### Ideal Answer

Replication lag is the delay between a write being committed on the leader and the same write being applied on followers. During this interval, replicas may return stale data.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why are read replicas useful?

### Ideal Answer

Read replicas distribute read traffic across multiple servers, reducing load on the leader and significantly increasing overall read throughput without affecting write ordering.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the replication strategy for a global social media platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use Leader–Follower replication with one primary handling writes and multiple read replicas distributed across regions. Employ asynchronous replication for low-latency writes, monitor replication lag, automatically promote replicas during failures using leader election, and route read-after-write requests to the primary when strong consistency is required.

# Revision Notes

- Leader–Follower replication uses one leader for writes and multiple followers for replication.
- Read replicas improve read scalability but do not increase write throughput.
- Synchronous replication provides stronger consistency but higher latency.
- Asynchronous replication offers better performance but introduces replication lag.
- Semi-synchronous replication balances durability and performance.
- Replication logs allow followers to catch up efficiently after failures.
- Automatic failover and leader election are essential for maintaining availability without creating split-brain scenarios.
