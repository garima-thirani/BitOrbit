# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why would a company choose Multi-Leader replication instead of Leader–Follower?

### Ideal Answer

Multi-Leader replication reduces write latency for globally distributed users by allowing writes in multiple regions. It improves regional availability but requires conflict detection and resolution because different leaders may accept conflicting updates concurrently.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the purpose of quorum in leaderless databases?

### Ideal Answer

Quorum ensures that read and write operations overlap on at least one replica. When configured correctly (R + W > RF), the system can provide strong consistency while remaining highly available.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is Read Repair?

### Ideal Answer

Read Repair detects stale replicas during read operations by comparing replica versions. If inconsistencies are found, the system automatically updates outdated replicas with the latest data.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why does Cassandra use Merkle Trees?

### Ideal Answer

Merkle Trees allow replicas to compare large datasets efficiently by exchanging hashes instead of every record. Only differing branches are synchronized, greatly reducing network traffic during repair operations.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the replication strategy for a globally distributed shopping platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use Leader–Follower replication for services requiring strict ordering, such as payment processing. For globally distributed user profiles or collaborative features, use Multi-Leader replication with conflict resolution. For highly available product catalog and session storage, use a leaderless database such as Cassandra with RF=3, W=2, and R=2, along with read repair, hinted handoff, anti-entropy, and monitoring to balance consistency, latency, and availability.

# Revision Notes

- Multi-Leader replication allows multiple writable regions but introduces conflict resolution challenges.
- Leaderless replication removes the dependency on a single leader and relies on quorum-based reads and writes.
- Replication Factor (RF) determines how many copies of data are maintained.
- Strong consistency in quorum systems is achieved when **R + W > RF**.
- Read Repair, Anti-Entropy, Hinted Handoff, and Merkle Trees are essential mechanisms for maintaining replica consistency.
- Cassandra and Dynamo popularized leaderless, quorum-based architectures for highly available distributed databases.
- Choosing between Leader–Follower, Multi-Leader, and Leaderless replication depends on latency, consistency, conflict tolerance, and operational complexity.
