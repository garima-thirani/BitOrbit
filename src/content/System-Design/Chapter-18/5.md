# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why do global databases use geo-partitioning instead of replicating all data everywhere?

### Ideal Answer

Geo-partitioning keeps frequently accessed data close to its users, reducing latency, lowering cross-region traffic, and helping organizations comply with data residency regulations. Replicating every dataset globally would significantly increase storage, bandwidth, and synchronization costs.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why are cross-shard transactions expensive?

### Ideal Answer

Cross-shard transactions require coordination between multiple independent databases. They often involve distributed transaction protocols such as Two-Phase Commit or Saga, increasing latency, network communication, and failure-handling complexity.

---

## Question 3

### Question

How does online rebalancing work?

### Ideal Answer

Online rebalancing incrementally copies partitions to new servers while continuing to serve production traffic. Once synchronization is complete, request routing is updated and the old copy is safely removed, avoiding downtime.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What is the purpose of a routing layer?

### Ideal Answer

The routing layer hides the physical distribution of data from applications. It consults metadata to determine the correct partition or region for each request and transparently forwards requests to the appropriate database.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the database architecture for a global ride-sharing platform operating in 100 countries.

### Ideal Answer

Deploy regional database clusters close to users with geo-partitioning based on city or region. Replicate critical metadata globally while keeping trip and user data local whenever possible. Use automatic routing, online rebalancing, and leader election for failover. Avoid cross-region transactions by designing workflows around local ownership and asynchronous event-driven communication. Reserve distributed transactions only for critical operations that truly require global consistency.

# Revision Notes

- Global databases distribute data across multiple geographic regions while presenting a single logical database.
- Geo-partitioning improves latency and supports data residency requirements by keeping data close to users.
- Cross-shard transactions require distributed coordination and should be minimized whenever possible.
- Scatter-gather queries are expensive because they require querying multiple shards and aggregating results.
- Online rebalancing and partition migration allow systems to scale without downtime.
- Metadata services and routing layers hide physical data placement from applications.
- Google Spanner, CockroachDB, DynamoDB Global Tables, MongoDB Atlas, and Vitess illustrate different approaches to building globally distributed databases.
