# Exponential Backoff

Never retry immediately.

Instead

wait longer

after every failure.

Example

```text
Retry 1

100 ms

↓

Retry 2

200 ms

↓

Retry 3

400 ms

↓

Retry 4

800 ms
```

This prevents

retry storms.

---

# Why Backoff?

Imagine

10,000 clients

retry instantly.

The failed service

becomes overloaded

even more.

Backoff spreads traffic.

# Circuit Breaker States

Three states exist.

```text
Closed

↓

Open

↓

Half Open
```

---

## Closed

Everything normal.

Requests flow.

---

## Open

Failures exceed threshold.

Requests stop.

Immediate failure returned.

---

## Half Open

After waiting

of requests.

If successful

close breaker.

Otherwise

open again.

# Bulkhead Pattern

Ships have

Distributed systems

---

Without Bulkhead

# Engineering Notes

## Retry is Not Reliability

Many engineers believe

adding retries

makes systems reliable.

Retries

without

timeouts,

backoff,

and circuit breakers

can make outages worse.

Retries amplify traffic.

Used incorrectly,

they create

retry storms.

---

## Every External Call Should Have a Plan

Before calling another service,

answer

"What happens if this call fails?"

Possible answers

- Retry
- Timeout
- Circuit Breaker
- Fallback
- Compensating Action

If you don't have an answer,

the design isn't complete.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why are retries alone dangerous?

### Ideal Answer

Retries increase traffic during failures. Without exponential backoff, jitter, and limits, they can overwhelm already struggling services and create retry storms. Retries should always be combined with timeouts and circuit breakers.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What problem does a Circuit Breaker solve?

### Ideal Answer

A Circuit Breaker prevents repeated calls to an unhealthy service. Instead of allowing requests to fail slowly, it temporarily blocks calls, reducing load and preventing cascading failures.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Explain the difference between Bulkhead and Circuit Breaker.

### Ideal Answer

A Circuit Breaker protects against repeatedly calling failing dependencies. A Bulkhead isolates resources so that failures in one subsystem (such as Payments) do not consume all threads or connections and impact unrelated services.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What is Graceful Degradation?

### Ideal Answer

Graceful Degradation means disabling or simplifying non-essential features during failures while keeping core business functionality available. For example, an e-commerce site may disable recommendations but continue processing orders.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design a resilient checkout system for a global e-commerce platform.

### Ideal Answer

Critical calls such as payment authorization use timeouts, retries with exponential backoff and jitter, and circuit breakers. Separate thread pools isolate dependencies using the Bulkhead pattern. Recommendation and analytics failures trigger fallbacks or graceful degradation rather than blocking checkout. Health checks remove unhealthy instances, monitoring tracks retry rates and breaker states, and chaos engineering validates resilience before production.
