# Engineering Notes

## JWT Is Not Encryption

A JWT is

Base64 encoded,

not encrypted.

Anyone possessing

the token

can read

its payload.

Never store

passwords

or sensitive information

inside a JWT.

---

## Internal Networks Are Not Trusted

Many real-world breaches

begin

inside

the infrastructure.

Modern Microservices

authenticate

every request,

even

between internal services.

This philosophy

is the foundation

of Zero Trust.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

What is the difference between Authentication and Authorization?

### Ideal Answer

Authentication verifies who a user or service is, while authorization determines what actions that authenticated identity is allowed to perform. Authentication happens first, followed by authorization.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why is JWT popular in Microservices?

### Ideal Answer

JWTs are stateless, compact, and easy to verify across multiple services without maintaining server-side session state. This makes them well suited for distributed systems.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What problem does mTLS solve?

### Ideal Answer

mTLS authenticates both the client and server using certificates while encrypting communication. It prevents unauthorized services from impersonating trusted services inside a Microservices environment.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What is Zero Trust?

### Ideal Answer

Zero Trust assumes that no network location is inherently trusted. Every user, service, and request must authenticate and authorize itself, regardless of whether it originates inside or outside the organization.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the security architecture for a large Microservices-based e-commerce platform.

### Ideal Answer

Authenticate users using OAuth2 with OIDC and issue short-lived JWTs. The API Gateway validates tokens before forwarding requests. Internal service-to-service communication uses mTLS with unique service identities. Secrets are stored in Vault with automatic rotation. RBAC governs user permissions, while Zero Trust principles ensure every request is authenticated and authorized. Encrypt all data in transit and at rest, and continuously audit access logs.

# Revision Notes

- Authentication identifies users and services.
- Authorization determines permissions.
- JWTs enable stateless authentication but should contain only non-sensitive claims.
- OAuth2 and OIDC provide secure identity and authorization flows.
- mTLS secures service-to-service communication.
- Vault centralizes secret storage and rotation.
- Zero Trust assumes no implicit trust, even within internal networks.
- Encrypt data both in transit and at rest.
- Apply the principle of least privilege to every identity.
