# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why can't Microservices use traditional ACID transactions?

### Ideal Answer

ACID transactions are designed for a single database. In Microservices, each service owns its own database, making global transactions impractical due to latency, coordination overhead, and availability concerns.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the Saga Pattern?

### Ideal Answer

A Saga is a sequence of local transactions where each successful step triggers the next. If a step fails, compensating transactions undo previously completed business operations instead of relying on database rollbacks.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is the difference between Choreography and Orchestration?

### Ideal Answer

In choreography, services coordinate by publishing and consuming events without a central controller. In orchestration, a dedicated orchestrator manages the workflow and explicitly tells each service what to do next.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why are compensating transactions necessary?

### Ideal Answer

Real-world actions such as charging a card or sending an email cannot be rolled back by a database transaction. Compensating transactions apply business logic to reverse or offset completed actions when a workflow fails.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the transaction flow for an e-commerce checkout involving Orders, Payments, Inventory, and Shipping.

### Ideal Answer

Use a Saga. The Order Service creates the order, Payment authorizes the charge, Inventory reserves stock, and Shipping creates the shipment. Each service performs a local transaction. If Inventory reservation fails after payment succeeds, execute compensating actions such as refunding the payment and canceling the order. Ensure retries are idempotent, include timeouts and DLQs, and monitor the saga lifecycle.

# Revision Notes

- ACID transactions do not scale across independent Microservice databases.
- BASE embraces eventual consistency for distributed systems.
- Sagas replace global transactions with coordinated local transactions.
- Compensating transactions reverse business effects rather than database state.
- Choreography favors decentralization; orchestration favors explicit control.
- Idempotency, retries, and monitoring are essential for reliable Sagas.
