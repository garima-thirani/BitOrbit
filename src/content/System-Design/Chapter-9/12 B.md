# Driver Matching Service

One of Uber's hardest engineering problems is matching riders with drivers.

Requirements

- Low latency (<100 ms)
- Millions of drivers online
- Constant GPS updates
- Fair driver allocation
- Minimize passenger waiting time

---

## Driver Matching Architecture

```text
Passenger Requests Ride

↓

RideRequested Event

↓

Kafka

↓

Matching Service

↓

Nearby Driver Search

↓

Ranking Engine

↓

DriverMatched Event

↓

Notification Service

↓

Driver App
```

Notice that the Ride Service never calls the Driver App directly.

Everything happens through events.

---

## Driver Ranking Factors

When multiple drivers are nearby, Uber considers:

- Distance to passenger
- Driver rating
- Estimated arrival time
- Current traffic
- Driver acceptance history
- Surge zone
- Driver fatigue rules
- Airport priority rules

These values continuously change.

# Failure Scenario

## Matching Service Failure

RideRequested

↓

Kafka

↓

Matching Service

↓

Crash

Kafka stores all events.

Matching Service restarts.

No ride request lost.

---

# Failure Scenario

## Pricing Failure

Pricing Service unavailable.

Ride requests continue entering Kafka.

Pricing resumes later.

Events replay automatically.

---

# Failure Scenario

## Notification Failure

Ride completed.

Receipt delayed.

Business operation still succeeds.

Notifications retry later.

# Why CQRS?

Drivers constantly request

```
Current Ride

↓

Nearby Requests

↓

Trip History

↓

Earnings
```

Reads vastly outnumber writes.

Read Models improve latency dramatically.

# Event Sourcing Usage

Not every Uber service uses Event Sourcing.

Typically

Financial domains

Driver payouts

Ride payments

Audit logs

maintain complete event history.

Location streams generally do not, due to their enormous volume.

# Lessons Learned

Uber demonstrates how Event-Driven Architecture enables:

- Independent scaling
- Low-latency ride matching
- Massive GPS event processing
- Fault isolation
- Replayable analytics
- Highly available customer-facing workflows

Unlike traditional request-response systems, Uber's business workflows continue even when non-critical services temporarily fail.
