# What is a Microservice?

A Microservice is

an independently deployable application

that owns

- its business logic
- its data
- its deployment
- its lifecycle

Example

```text
Orders

Payments

Inventory

Shipping

Search

Recommendations
```

Each service exists independently.

---

# High-Level Microservice Architecture

```text

                         │

─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────

│         │         │         │         │

▼         ▼         ▼         ▼         ▼

Orders Payments Inventory Search Notifications

│         │         │         │         │

```

Notice

Every service owns

# Why Independent Databases?

Orders suddenly fails.

Shared databases create

hidden coupling.

Microservices should own

# When NOT to Use Microservices

Do NOT use Microservices if you have

- Small engineering team
- Startup MVP
- Internal tool
- CRUD application
- Limited infrastructure
- No DevOps culture

A modular monolith is often a better solution.

# Engineering Notes

## Monolith First

Nearly every successful technology company—including Amazon, Netflix, Facebook, Shopify, and GitHub—started with a monolith or a tightly integrated application before evolving into distributed systems.

The goal is not to avoid monoliths.

The goal is to outgrow them.

---

## Migration Takes Years

Microservice migrations rarely happen in one project.

Typical migration timeline

```text
Monolith

↓

Authentication

↓

Payments

↓

Search

↓

Notifications

↓

Analytics

↓

Remaining Core
```

Many companies spend

3–8 years

completing this evolution.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why did the industry move from Monoliths to Microservices?

### Ideal Answer

As engineering teams and user bases grew, monoliths became difficult to deploy, scale, and maintain. Microservices allow independent deployment, independent scaling, better team ownership, and improved fault isolation, making them better suited for large organizations.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is Conway's Law?

### Ideal Answer

Conway's Law states that software architecture mirrors an organization's communication structure. Independent teams naturally build independent services, while tightly coupled organizations tend to build tightly coupled software.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Should every company use Microservices?

### Ideal Answer

No. Microservices are beneficial for large organizations with multiple teams and scaling challenges. Small teams and startups usually benefit more from a modular monolith because it is simpler to develop, deploy, and operate.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why should each Microservice own its database?

### Ideal Answer

Owning its database prevents tight coupling between services. Other services interact through APIs or events instead of directly accessing another service's data, allowing schemas and deployments to evolve independently.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Your company has grown from 10 engineers to 500 engineers. How would you migrate from a monolith to Microservices?

### Ideal Answer

Avoid a full rewrite. Start with a modular monolith, identify stable business capabilities, and gradually extract services using the Strangler Fig Pattern. Begin with domains like authentication, payments, and search, introduce API gateways and asynchronous communication where appropriate, and ensure each extracted service owns its data and deployment lifecycle.

# Revision Notes

- Monoliths are the correct starting point for most startups.
- Microservices solve organizational and scaling problems, not just technical ones.
- Conway's Law explains why team structure influences architecture.
- Each service should own its business capability, deployment, and data.
- Migrations should be incremental using the Strangler Fig Pattern.
- Microservices increase operational complexity and should be adopted only when justified.
