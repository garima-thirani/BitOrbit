# Decision Framework

Ask these questions before creating a new service.

| Question | If YES |
|-----------|---------|
| Different business capability? | Consider new service |
| Independent deployment needed? | Separate service |
| Different scaling profile? | Separate service |
| Different ownership team? | Separate service |
| Different lifecycle? | Separate service |

If most answers are "No",

keep it inside the existing service.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

What is a Bounded Context?

### Ideal Answer

A Bounded Context defines the boundary within which a particular domain model is valid. The same business term can have different meanings in different contexts, allowing services to evolve independently without forcing a single global model.

---

## Question 2

### Question

How do you decide Microservice boundaries?

### Ideal Answer

Microservice boundaries should follow business capabilities rather than technical layers. A good candidate service has distinct business rules, independent ownership, independent scaling requirements, and its own lifecycle.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is the difference between an Entity and a Value Object?

### Ideal Answer

An Entity has a unique identity that persists even if its attributes change, while a Value Object is defined entirely by its values and is usually immutable. Customers and Orders are Entities; Addresses and Money are common Value Objects.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why is an Anti-Corruption Layer useful?

### Ideal Answer

An Anti-Corruption Layer isolates a modern bounded context from legacy models by translating between them. This prevents legacy concepts from leaking into the new domain model and keeps the service clean and maintainable.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Your company has a massive User Service containing profiles, payments, wishlists, addresses, orders, and loyalty points. How would you redesign it?

### Ideal Answer

First identify distinct business capabilities using DDD. Extract services incrementally using the Strangler Fig Pattern—for example, Orders, Payments, Loyalty, and Profiles—each with its own bounded context, database, and APIs/events. Avoid a big-bang rewrite, preserve backward compatibility through an Anti-Corruption Layer where necessary, and align service ownership with dedicated teams.

# Revision Notes

- Design services around business capabilities, not technical layers.
- DDD provides a vocabulary for finding good service boundaries.
- Bounded Contexts prevent a single bloated domain model.
- Entities have identity; Value Objects do not.
- Aggregates enforce business consistency.
- Anti-Corruption Layers protect modern services from legacy systems.
- Service granularity is a balance—avoid both monoliths and microservice sprawl.
