# Introduction

YouTube is one of the largest distributed systems ever built.

Every minute, users upload hundreds of hours of video.

At the same time,

billions of videos are streamed worldwide.

Unlike Netflix,

YouTube must solve **both**:

- Massive video uploads
- Massive video streaming

This makes it significantly more challenging than a pure streaming platform.

---

# Core Requirements

Users should be able to:

- Upload videos
- Watch videos
- Search videos
- Like/Comment
- Subscribe
- Receive recommendations
- Watch live streams

# Video Upload Pipeline

Uploading a video is an asynchronous workflow.

```text
User Uploads Video

↓

Chunk Upload

↓

Temporary Storage

↓

Kafka

↓

Video Processing

↓

Object Storage

↓

CDN

↓

Published
```

The user doesn't wait for processing to finish.

---

# Chunked Upload

Large videos are divided into chunks.

```text
5 GB Video

↓

Chunk 1

Chunk 2

Chunk 3

...

Chunk N
```

Benefits:

- Resume uploads
- Parallel uploads
- Lower retry cost

# Why Transcoding?

Users have different devices.

One uploaded video becomes many versions.

Example:

```text
Original

↓

1080p

↓

720p

↓

480p

↓

360p

↓

240p
```

Each version is optimized for different devices and network speeds.

---

# Adaptive Bitrate Streaming (ABR)

Suppose network quality changes.

Without ABR:

```text
Video Buffers
```

With ABR:

```text
1080p

↓

720p

↓

480p

↓

360p
```

The player automatically switches quality.

Protocols:

- HLS
- MPEG-DASH

```text

```


# Common Interview Questions

### Why chunk uploads?

To support resumable uploads and reduce retry costs.

---

### Why transcode videos?

Different devices and networks require different resolutions and bitrates.

---

### Why use a CDN?

Video bandwidth is enormous.

Serving videos from edge locations reduces latency and origin load.

---

### Why is YouTube harder than Netflix?

Netflix stores professionally produced content.

YouTube must additionally support:

- User uploads
- Video processing
- Transcoding
- Copyright detection
- Recommendations
- Live streaming

---

### Why separate metadata from video data?

Metadata changes frequently.

Video bytes rarely change.

Separating them improves scalability.

# Real-World Example

Imagine a television station.

Before broadcasting a program, it:

- Receives the raw recording.
- Converts it into multiple broadcast formats.
- Creates previews.
- Distributes copies to regional stations.
- Broadcasts from the nearest station to viewers.

YouTube follows the same workflow, except it performs this process for **millions of videos every day**, automatically.

# Key Takeaways

- YouTube combines **video upload, processing, storage, streaming, search, and recommendation** into one distributed system.
- Chunked uploads improve reliability and enable resumable transfers.
- Videos are transcoded into multiple resolutions to support adaptive bitrate streaming.
- CDNs cache popular content close to users, reducing latency and origin bandwidth.
- Kafka decouples upload, processing, analytics, and notifications into asynchronous pipelines.
- Recommendation systems drive most video consumption and rely heavily on machine learning.
- Unlike Netflix, YouTube must process user-generated content before it can be streamed, making its architecture significantly more complex.
