# Delivery Tracking

Sending a notification does **not** mean it was delivered.

Example:

```text
Notification Sent

↓

Phone Offline

↓

Not Delivered
```

The system should track every stage.

---

# Notification Lifecycle

A notification progresses through multiple states.

```text
Created

↓

Queued

↓

Sent

↓

Delivered

↓

Opened
```

Failure states include:

```text
Created

↓

Failed

↓

Retrying

↓

Dead Letter Queue
```

Using explicit states simplifies monitoring and debugging.

---

# Why Track Delivery?

Suppose a bank sends:

```text
₹10,000 Debited
```

The customer never receives it.

Did the gateway fail?

Was the phone offline?

Did the provider reject the message?

Without delivery tracking,

it's impossible to know.

# Trade-offs

## Kafka

Advantages:

- Decouples producers and consumers
- Handles traffic spikes
- Durable event storage

Disadvantages:

- Operational complexity

---

## Retry

Advantages:

- Recovers transient failures

Disadvantages:

- May create duplicate deliveries if idempotency isn't implemented

---

## Dead Letter Queue

Advantages:

- Prevents message loss
- Enables debugging

Disadvantages:

- Requires operational monitoring

---

## Batching

Advantages:

- Better user experience
- Lower delivery cost

Disadvantages:

- Slight delivery delay

---

## Multi-Region

Advantages:

- Low latency
- Disaster recovery

Disadvantages:

- Increased operational complexity

# Common Interview Questions

---

## Why use Kafka?

Kafka decouples applications from notification delivery, allowing workers to process messages asynchronously and absorb traffic spikes.

---

## Why Retry?

Network failures and provider outages are often temporary.

Retries recover many failed deliveries automatically.

---

## Why a Dead Letter Queue?

Notifications that repeatedly fail are moved to a DLQ for investigation rather than being discarded.

---

## Why Rate Limiting?

Rate limiting prevents notification storms caused by bugs, malicious users, or repeated retries.

---

## Why Separate Workers?

Push, Email, and SMS providers have different APIs, throughput limits, and retry policies.

Independent workers allow each channel to scale separately.

# Real-World Example

Imagine a postal distribution center.

Letters first arrive at a sorting facility.

They are grouped based on delivery method:

- Local delivery
- Air mail
- International courier

If a delivery fails,

the letter is retried.

If repeated attempts fail,

it is placed in a special investigation bin.

A Notification Service follows the same workflow using queues, workers, retries, and Dead Letter Queues.

# Key Takeaways

- Notification Services centralize message delivery for Push, Email, SMS, and scheduled notifications.
- Kafka enables reliable, asynchronous processing and absorbs traffic spikes.
- Delivery tracking records each notification's lifecycle from creation to user interaction.
- Rate limiting and batching protect users from notification fatigue and reduce operational costs.
- Retry mechanisms and Dead Letter Queues improve reliability while preserving failed messages for investigation.
- Multi-region deployment, replication, and monitoring ensure high availability and low latency.
- Notification systems demonstrate how event-driven architectures, queues, and specialized workers combine to deliver billions of messages reliably.
