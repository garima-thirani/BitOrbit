# Settlement

Settlement is the process of actually transferring money from the customer's bank to the merchant's bank.

Unlike authorization,

settlement is usually **batched**.

---

# Why Batch Settlement?

Imagine Amazon processes

```text
5 Million

Payments

Per Day
```

Transferring money immediately for every transaction would overload banking systems.

Instead,

banks group payments.

```text
Payments

↓

Batch

↓

Settlement

↓

Merchant
```

---

# Settlement Flow

```text
Customer

↓

Issuing Bank

↓

Card Network

↓

Acquiring Bank

↓

Merchant Bank

↓

Merchant Account
```

Eventually,

the merchant receives the funds.

---

# Settlement Timeline

Example:

```text

Merchant Receives Money
```

Different payment methods have different settlement times.

# Reconciliation

Reconciliation ensures

the financial records of all parties match.

Example:

Merchant says:

```text
100 Payments
```

Gateway says:

```text
100 Payments
```

Bank says:

```text
100 Payments
```

Everything matches.

Perfect.

---

# Why Reconciliation Matters

Suppose:

Merchant database:

```text
₹500
```

Bank:

```text
₹0
```

Something failed.

The system must discover the mismatch.

---

```text
↓

↓

```

No transaction should remain unresolved.

```text

↓
```

```text
↓

Processor
```

---

# Why New Transactions?

Financial records should be immutable.

Instead of:

```text
Payment

$100

↓

$0
```

Create:

```text
Payment

$100

+

Refund

-$100
```

Complete audit history remains intact.

# Fraud Detection

Payment fraud causes billions of dollars in losses every year.

The gateway must identify suspicious transactions before approving them.

Examples include:

- New device
- New country
- Impossible travel
- Too many payments
- Blacklisted card
- Velocity attacks

No single signal is sufficient.

# Trade-offs

## Immediate Settlement

Advantages:

- Faster merchant payouts

Disadvantages:

- Higher banking overhead

---

## Batch Settlement

Advantages:

- Lower cost
- Better throughput

Disadvantages:

- Delayed merchant payments

---

## Synchronous Fraud Checks

Advantages:

- Immediate decision

Disadvantages:

- Increased latency

---

## Asynchronous Analytics

Advantages:

- Fast checkout

Disadvantages:

- Eventually consistent reporting

# Common Interview Questions

---

## Why use Idempotency?

To prevent duplicate charges caused by:

- Double-clicks
- Client retries
- Network timeouts
- Gateway retries

---

## Why Double-Entry Ledger?

Ensures every debit has a corresponding credit,

making financial records auditable and mathematically consistent.

---

## Why Reconciliation?

Distributed systems can fail.

Reconciliation detects inconsistencies between:

- Gateway
- Processor
- Bank
- Merchant

---

## Why Separate Settlement?

Authorization confirms payment.

Settlement actually transfers funds.

Separating them improves banking efficiency.

---

## Why Tokenization?

Reduces PCI scope,

improves security,

and prevents storage of sensitive card numbers.

# Key Takeaways

- Payment gateways orchestrate communication between merchants and financial institutions but do not move money directly.
- Authorization, capture, settlement, and reconciliation are distinct stages in the payment lifecycle.
- Idempotency prevents duplicate charges and is one of the most important concepts in payment systems.
- Immutable ledgers and double-entry accounting provide correctness, auditability, and financial integrity.
- Fraud detection combines rule-based systems and machine learning to evaluate transaction risk.
- PCI-DSS, encryption, and tokenization protect sensitive payment information.
- High availability, replication, monitoring, and asynchronous processing enable payment systems to operate reliably at internet scale.
- Financial systems prioritize **correctness and durability over raw performance**, making them some of the most demanding distributed systems to design.
