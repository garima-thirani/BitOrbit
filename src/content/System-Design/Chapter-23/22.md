# Step 8 — Explain Ride Assignment

Interviewers frequently ask:

> **How do you prevent assigning one driver to two passengers?**

Answer:

Acquire a distributed lock.

```text
Driver

↓

Redis Lock

↓

Notify Driver

↓

Accepted?

↓

Unlock
```

This prevents race conditions.

# Common Interview Questions

---

## Why Redis?

Because location updates change continuously.

Redis provides:

- Fast writes
- Fast reads
- GEO indexing

Ideal for live locations.

---

## Why Kafka?

Ride events trigger multiple downstream systems.

Kafka enables:

- Loose coupling
- Retry
- Event replay
- Independent scaling

---

## Why H3?

Because location searches become extremely efficient.

Benefits:

- Hexagonal cells
- Equal-distance neighbors
- Simple proximity search
- Better spatial partitioning

---

## Why WebSockets?

Polling GPS would waste bandwidth.

Persistent connections enable:

- Live tracking
- Driver notifications
- ETA updates

---

## How Do You Handle Driver Rejects?

Simple.

```text
Reject

↓

Unlock Driver

↓

Choose Next Driver
```

The passenger usually experiences only a small delay.

---

## What Happens If Driver Disconnects?

The driver's session expires.

Ride monitoring detects lost updates.

Passengers are notified if necessary.

---

## How Does ETA Work?

ETA uses:

- GPS
- Road Network
- Traffic
- Historical Data
- Machine Learning

Distance alone isn't sufficient.

---

## How Does Surge Pricing Work?

Demand and supply are continuously monitored.

Higher demand increases prices.

As more drivers become available,

prices normalize.

---

## How Would You Support Shared Rides?

Introduce:

- Route Optimization
- Passenger Clustering
- Multiple Pickups
- Capacity Constraints

The core architecture remains unchanged.

# Alternative Architectures

---

## SQL Only

Advantages

- Simple
- ACID

Disadvantages

- Poor GPS scaling
- Slow nearby searches

---

## NoSQL

Advantages

- Horizontal scaling
- High write throughput

Disadvantages

- Eventual consistency

---

## Monolith

Advantages

- Easy development

Disadvantages

- Difficult scaling

---

## Microservices

Advantages

- Independent deployment
- Independent scaling

Disadvantages

- Operational complexity

Uber chooses microservices because of its scale.

---

# Common Interview Mistakes

---

## Mistake 1

Ignoring GPS updates.

Location tracking is Uber's core workload.

---

## Mistake 2

Using SQL for nearby driver searches.

Geospatial indexing is essential.

---

## Mistake 3

Ignoring race conditions.

Multiple passengers may compete for one driver.

Distributed locking is required.

---

## Mistake 4

Ignoring ETA.

Distance is not enough.

Traffic matters.

---

## Mistake 5

Ignoring surge pricing.

Dynamic pricing is a core business feature.

---

## Mistake 6

Treating payments as synchronous.

Payments should be handled by dedicated services.

# Key Takeaways

- Uber is fundamentally a **real-time geospatial matching platform**.
- Geospatial indexing enables millisecond driver searches without scanning millions of records.
- Distributed locking prevents multiple riders from being assigned the same driver.
- Kafka powers asynchronous workflows including notifications, payments, and analytics.
- Live GPS tracking relies on WebSockets and continuous location updates.
- ETA prediction and surge pricing combine real-time data with machine learning.
- Multi-region deployment and observability ensure global reliability and scalability.
- Uber showcases how distributed systems, cloud infrastructure, event streaming, caching, and AI work together to power one of the world's largest transportation platforms.
