# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why is Cache-Aside the most common caching strategy?

### Ideal Answer

Cache-Aside gives the application full control over cache population and invalidation. It is simple, flexible, works well with Redis, and is highly effective for read-heavy workloads.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the difference between Write-Through and Write-Back caching?

### Ideal Answer

Write-Through updates both the cache and database before acknowledging the write, ensuring strong consistency. Write-Back acknowledges immediately after updating the cache and writes to the database asynchronously, improving write performance at the cost of eventual consistency.

---

## Question 3

### Question

When should Write-Around caching be used?

### Ideal Answer

Write-Around is useful for write-heavy workloads where recently written data is unlikely to be read immediately. It prevents unnecessary cache pollution by writing directly to the database.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What problem does Refresh-Ahead solve?

### Ideal Answer

Refresh-Ahead proactively reloads frequently accessed cache entries before they expire, reducing cache misses and maintaining consistently low latency for popular data.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the caching strategy for a global e-commerce platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use Cache-Aside for product pages, categories, and recommendations because these are read frequently. Use Write-Through for inventory and pricing to maintain consistency. Use Write-Back for analytics and clickstream events to reduce database load. Refresh popular products proactively using Refresh-Ahead, monitor cache hit ratio and latency, and invalidate cached entries whenever business-critical data changes.

# Revision Notes

- Cache-Aside is the most widely used caching strategy.
- Read-Through delegates cache loading to the cache layer.
- Write-Through prioritizes consistency by updating cache and database together.
- Write-Back prioritizes write performance through asynchronous persistence.
- Write-Around avoids unnecessary cache entries for write-heavy workloads.
- Refresh-Ahead proactively refreshes popular entries before expiration.
- Large production systems typically combine multiple cache patterns rather than relying on a single approach.
