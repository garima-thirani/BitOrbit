# Production Case Study 3

# Netflix Streaming Platform

> **"Netflix is not just a video streaming service. It is one of the world's largest event-processing platforms. Every click, play, pause, seek, recommendation, and rating becomes an event."**

---

# Problem Statement

Design Netflix's backend capable of supporting

- 300+ Million Users
- Global Streaming
- Personalized Recommendations
- Continue Watching
- Trending
- Billing
- Device Synchronization
- Analytics
- Content Ranking

The system should support

- Millions of concurrent streams
- Billions of playback events/day
- Near real-time recommendations
- Multi-device synchronization

---

# Functional Requirements

Users should be able to

- Browse movies
- Search content
- Play videos
- Pause
- Resume
- Seek
- Stop playback
- Rate content
- Continue watching on another device

Internal systems should

- Generate recommendations
- Track viewing history
- Detect trending shows
- Produce analytics
- Train ML models

---

# Non-Functional Requirements

Availability

99.99%

---

Playback startup

<2 seconds

---

Recommendation latency

<200ms

---

Massive scalability

Billions of playback events/day

---

Fault tolerance

Playback should continue even if recommendation systems fail.

---

# Why Event-Driven?

Imagine playback directly calls

```

↓

Analytics

↓

Recommendations

↓

Watch History

↓

Trending

↓

Notifications
```

Playback latency becomes terrible.

Instead

Playback records

```

```

Everything else happens asynchronously.

# Event Sourcing

Netflix generally does **not**

event-source raw playback events forever.

Reason

Storage cost.

# Activity Lifecycle

```text
PostCreated

↓

LikeAdded

↓

CommentAdded

↓

↓

ProfileViewed

↓

ConnectionAccepted
```

Everything is represented as immutable events.

---

# Why Events?

Suppose Alice likes Bob's post.

Immediately

The following systems need updates:

- Feed Ranking
- Notifications
- Analytics
- Ads
- Search
- Recommendation Engine

Instead of direct service-to-service calls,

Activity Service publishes

```
LikeAdded
```

Kafka distributes the event.

Every downstream service reacts independently.

---

