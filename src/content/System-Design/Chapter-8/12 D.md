# Feed Ranking Engine

The LinkedIn feed is one of the most sophisticated recommendation systems in the industry.

Whenever a member performs an action

```
Create Post

↓

Like

↓

Comment

↓

Share

↓

Follow

↓

Connect
```

the feed ranking continuously changes.

---

## Feed Architecture

```text
Member Activity

↓

Activity Service

↓

Kafka

↓

Feed Projection

↓

Ranking Engine

↓

Redis Timeline Cache

↓

Feed API
```

Notice

The Activity Service never computes rankings.

It only publishes events.

---

# Feed Ranking Signals

LinkedIn ranks posts using hundreds of signals.

Examples include

- Relationship strength
- Previous interactions
- Industry similarity
- Company similarity
- Geographic proximity
- Engagement probability
- Post freshness
- Spam probability
- Member interests

Every signal originates from historical events.

# Production Case Study 8

# Stock Exchange

---

# Requirements

Support

- Millions of trades/sec
- Ultra-low latency
- High availability
- Strong ordering
- Regulatory audit

---

# Event Flow

```text
OrderPlaced

↓

OrderMatched

↓

TradeExecuted

↓

SettlementStarted

↓

SettlementCompleted
```

---

# Matching Engine

Critical path.

Usually

NOT

Kafka.

Instead

High-performance in-memory engine.

Kafka receives

completed trades.

---

# Why?

Matching

requires

microsecond latency.

Kafka

is excellent

for downstream distribution,

not matching itself.

---

# Downstream Consumers

TradeExecuted

↓

Kafka

↓

Risk

↓

Analytics

↓

Settlement

↓

Compliance

↓

Market Data

---

# Lessons Learned

Separate

Latency-critical

processing

from

event distribution.

Not everything belongs inside Kafka.
