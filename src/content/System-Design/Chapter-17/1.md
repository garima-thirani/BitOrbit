# Engineering Notes

## Distributed Systems Must Embrace Failure

Unlike

Servers crash,

Good architectures

## Consistency Is a Business Decision

Choosing

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why are distributed transactions significantly harder than single database transactions?

### Ideal Answer

Distributed transactions involve multiple independent services and databases connected through unreliable networks. Failures, timeouts, partial commits, and network partitions make coordination much more complex than transactions within a single database.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why do modern microservice architectures often choose BASE instead of ACID?

### Ideal Answer

Global ACID transactions across many services are expensive and reduce scalability and availability. BASE allows systems to remain available during failures and eventually converge to a consistent state through asynchronous communication and compensation.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is eventual consistency?

### Ideal Answer

Eventual consistency guarantees that if no new updates occur, all replicas will eventually converge to the same state. Temporary inconsistencies are acceptable in exchange for higher availability and scalability.

---

## Question 4

### Question

When should ACID be preferred over BASE?

### Ideal Answer

ACID should be used when correctness is critical, such as banking, payment ledgers, or financial transactions. BASE is appropriate when temporary inconsistency is acceptable, such as social media feeds, analytics, and recommendation systems.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the transaction strategy for a global e-commerce platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use ACID transactions within each individual service database, such as payment processing or inventory reservation. Coordinate workflows across services using event-driven communication, eventual consistency, retries, compensating transactions, and Saga patterns instead of distributed ACID transactions. This balances correctness with scalability and fault tolerance.

# Revision Notes

- Distributed transactions span multiple independent services and databases.
- ACID provides strong consistency guarantees for single-database transactions.
- BASE prioritizes availability and eventual consistency for distributed systems.
- Partial failures and network partitions make distributed transactions challenging.
- Modern microservice architectures typically combine local ACID transactions with global BASE workflows.
- Eventual consistency is a deliberate design choice, not a system failure.
- Selecting ACID or BASE depends on business requirements and acceptable consistency trade-offs.
