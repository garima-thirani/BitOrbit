# Engineering Notes

## Atomicity Has a Cost

Two-Phase Commit

## Coordination Does Not Scale Easily

As

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why is the Prepare phase necessary in Two-Phase Commit?

### Ideal Answer

The Prepare phase ensures every participant confirms it can successfully commit before any participant permanently commits changes. This prevents partial commits and maintains atomicity across distributed systems.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the biggest weakness of Two-Phase Commit?

### Ideal Answer

The biggest weakness is blocking. If the coordinator fails after participants enter the prepared state, participants must wait indefinitely while holding locks until the coordinator recovers or the transaction can be resolved.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why is Three-Phase Commit rarely used in production?

### Ideal Answer

Although Three-Phase Commit reduces blocking compared to Two-Phase Commit, it assumes bounded network delays and synchronized timing, assumptions that are difficult to guarantee in real-world distributed systems. Modern architectures generally prefer Saga patterns or consensus algorithms.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why do internet-scale microservices avoid Two-Phase Commit?

### Ideal Answer

Two-Phase Commit introduces high latency, long-held locks, blocking during failures, and poor scalability as the number of participating services increases. Event-driven workflows with compensating transactions provide better scalability and availability.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the transaction coordination mechanism for a banking transfer involving multiple databases.

### Ideal Answer

Use Two-Phase Commit when strong atomicity across participating databases is required. A coordinator initiates the Prepare phase, gathers votes from all participants, and commits only if every participant responds positively. Maintain transaction logs for recovery, configure timeouts, monitor coordinator health, and understand that the blocking nature of 2PC makes it appropriate only for relatively small, strongly consistent distributed transactions.

# Revision Notes

- Two-Phase Commit coordinates distributed transactions using a Prepare phase followed by a Commit phase.
- The coordinator collects votes from participants before making a global commit decision.
- Participants lock resources and log their state during the Prepare phase.
- If any participant votes NO, the coordinator instructs all participants to roll back.
- Coordinator failures can block participants, making blocking the primary weakness of 2PC.
- Three-Phase Commit adds a Pre-Commit phase to reduce uncertainty but is rarely used in modern internet-scale systems.
- Large distributed systems generally favor Saga patterns and asynchronous coordination over global distributed commits.
