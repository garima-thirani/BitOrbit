# Engineering Notes

## Sagas Replace Atomicity with Business Recovery

Unlike

Two-Phase Commit,

Sagas

do not guarantee

that

all services

commit together.

Instead,

they guarantee

that

the business process

can recover

through

compensating actions.

This shifts

the focus

from

database consistency

to

business consistency.

---

## Compensation Is Business Logic

A compensation

transaction

is not

a database rollback.

It is

a new,

valid

business operation

that reverses

the previous action.

For example,

refunding

a payment

creates

a new financial transaction

rather than

deleting

the original payment.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why is the Saga Pattern preferred over Two-Phase Commit in microservices?

### Ideal Answer

Saga avoids global locks and blocking by allowing each service to commit its own local transaction independently. If a later step fails, compensating transactions restore business consistency. This approach scales significantly better than Two-Phase Commit.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is a compensation transaction?

### Ideal Answer

A compensation transaction is a new business operation that reverses the effects of a previously committed transaction. Examples include refunding a payment, releasing reserved inventory, or cancelling a hotel reservation.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is the difference between choreography and orchestration?

### Ideal Answer

In choreography, services coordinate themselves by publishing and consuming events without a central controller. In orchestration, a dedicated orchestrator controls the workflow and instructs services which actions to perform.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why must compensation transactions be idempotent?

### Ideal Answer

Distributed systems may retry compensation requests due to failures or timeouts. Idempotent compensation ensures repeated execution does not create incorrect business outcomes such as issuing multiple refunds.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the transaction workflow for a global e-commerce checkout.

### Ideal Answer

Each microservice executes a local ACID transaction. The checkout workflow is coordinated using a Saga pattern. Payment, inventory, shipping, and notification services communicate through events or an orchestrator. Failures trigger compensating actions such as payment refunds and inventory release. Retries, idempotency keys, timeouts, dead-letter queues, and monitoring ensure reliability and eventual consistency without relying on global distributed transactions.

# Revision Notes

- The Saga Pattern coordinates distributed business workflows using local transactions.
- Each service commits independently and owns its own database.
- Compensation transactions reverse business effects instead of rolling back database transactions.
- Choreography uses events for decentralized coordination, while orchestration uses a central controller.
- Sagas provide eventual consistency without global locks.
- Compensation logic must be idempotent and support retries.
- Sagas are the preferred transaction model for large-scale microservice architectures.
