# Engineering Notes

## Consensus Solves Agreement, Not Storage

Consensus algorithms

do not

by themselves.

Instead,

they ensure

that

agrees

on

the same sequence

of decisions.

Storage systems

build

on top

to provide

---

## Majority Is the Foundation of Reliability

Consensus relies

on

majority agreement,

not

unanimous agreement.

This allows

despite

individual node failures,

while preventing

multiple leaders

from making

conflicting decisions.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why is consensus required in distributed systems?

### Ideal Answer

Consensus ensures that distributed nodes agree on a single sequence of decisions despite failures, preventing conflicting data, split-brain scenarios, and inconsistent system state.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is quorum, and why is it important?

### Ideal Answer

A quorum is a majority of nodes, calculated as (N/2) + 1. It guarantees that two conflicting leaders cannot both obtain a majority, preserving safety and preventing split-brain conditions.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why is Raft generally preferred over Paxos?

### Ideal Answer

Raft provides the same safety guarantees as Paxos while using a simpler leader-based design that is easier to understand, implement, debug, and teach. As a result, many modern distributed systems adopt Raft.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What is split brain?

### Ideal Answer

Split brain occurs when multiple nodes incorrectly believe they are the leader and accept writes simultaneously, creating divergent system state. Consensus algorithms prevent split brain through majority quorum and leader election.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the coordination mechanism for a distributed configuration service.

### Ideal Answer

Deploy an odd number of nodes using the Raft consensus algorithm. Elect a single leader through majority voting. The leader accepts all configuration updates, appends them to its log, replicates entries to followers, and commits changes after receiving acknowledgments from a quorum. Followers apply committed entries in the same order, ensuring strong consistency and automatic failover during leader failures.

# Revision Notes

- Consensus enables distributed systems to agree on a single sequence of decisions.
- Leader election ensures only one node accepts writes at a time.
- Majority quorum prevents conflicting leaders and split brain.
- Log replication synchronizes data across replicas.
- Paxos introduced practical distributed consensus but is difficult to implement.
- Raft simplifies consensus through explicit leader election and log replication.
- Modern systems such as etcd, Consul, CockroachDB, TiDB, and Kafka's KRaft use Raft-based coordination.
