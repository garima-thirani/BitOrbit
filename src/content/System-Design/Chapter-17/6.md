# Engineering Notes

## Production Systems Mix Multiple Coordination Techniques

Real-world systems

rarely rely

on

a single mechanism.

For example,

Kubernetes

uses

Raft

for cluster metadata,

while

applications

running on Kubernetes

often use

Saga patterns

for

business workflows.

The infrastructure

and

the application

solve

different coordination problems.

---

## Strong Consistency Should Be Applied Selectively

Consensus protocols

provide

excellent correctness,

but

they introduce

extra network hops,

majority voting,

and replication delays.

Most large systems

apply

strong consistency

only

to

critical metadata,

while allowing

business workflows

to use

eventual consistency

where appropriate.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why does Google Spanner use TrueTime?

### Ideal Answer

TrueTime provides tightly bounded clock uncertainty using GPS and atomic clocks, allowing Spanner to assign globally ordered timestamps and achieve externally consistent distributed transactions across data centers.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why does Kubernetes use etcd?

### Ideal Answer

Kubernetes stores all cluster metadata—including Pods, Deployments, Services, Secrets, and ConfigMaps—in etcd because it provides strongly consistent, highly available storage using the Raft consensus algorithm.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why do companies like Amazon avoid Two-Phase Commit?

### Ideal Answer

Two-Phase Commit introduces blocking, long-held locks, and poor scalability. Amazon instead uses Saga workflows, local ACID transactions, and asynchronous event-driven communication to achieve business consistency.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What role does Raft play in CockroachDB?

### Ideal Answer

Raft replicates data across replicas, elects leaders, and ensures that writes are committed only after receiving acknowledgments from a majority of replicas, providing strong consistency and automatic failover.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the coordination layer for a globally distributed cloud platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use an odd-sized Raft-based etcd cluster for metadata and configuration management. Employ leader election, quorum-based replication, watches for configuration changes, distributed locks for critical coordination, and Saga workflows for business transactions. Reserve strong consensus for infrastructure metadata while allowing application workflows to use event-driven eventual consistency for scalability.

# Revision Notes

- Google Spanner combines Paxos with TrueTime to achieve globally consistent SQL transactions.
- CockroachDB uses Raft groups to replicate distributed key ranges with automatic failover.
- Kubernetes relies on etcd to store strongly consistent cluster metadata.
- etcd stores infrastructure metadata rather than business data.
- Stripe combines local ACID transactions with Saga workflows and idempotency.
- Amazon and Uber use Saga-based compensation instead of global distributed transactions.
- Modern distributed systems apply consensus to infrastructure coordination and event-driven workflows to business processes.
