# Engineering Notes

## Coordination Services Store Metadata, Not Business Data

Systems

such as

ZooKeeper

and etcd

are optimized

for

small,

high-value metadata,

including

leaders,

configuration,

service locations,

and locks.

Large datasets

belong

in databases,

not

coordination systems.

---

## Distributed Locks Should Be Used Sparingly

Distributed locks

solve

critical coordination problems,

but

they also

introduce

latency

and reduce

parallelism.

Whenever possible,

prefer

idempotent operations,

database constraints,

or optimistic concurrency

before

introducing

distributed locking.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why do distributed systems need coordination services like ZooKeeper or etcd?

### Ideal Answer

Coordination services provide strongly consistent shared metadata for leader election, distributed locking, service discovery, configuration management, and cluster membership. They ensure multiple distributed nodes make consistent coordination decisions despite failures.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the purpose of an ephemeral node in ZooKeeper?

### Ideal Answer

An ephemeral node exists only while the client session remains active. If the client disconnects or crashes, the node is automatically deleted, enabling automatic lock release and leader failure detection.

---

## Question 3

### Question

How does etcd differ from ZooKeeper?

### Ideal Answer

ZooKeeper organizes data as a hierarchical tree of ZNodes and traditionally uses the Zab protocol, while etcd uses a flat key-value model backed by the Raft consensus algorithm. etcd is the standard coordination store for Kubernetes.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why are distributed locks necessary?

### Ideal Answer

Distributed locks ensure that only one process accesses a shared resource at a time, preventing duplicate processing, race conditions, and inconsistent updates across multiple servers.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the coordination layer for a Kubernetes-like container orchestration platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use a Raft-based etcd cluster with an odd number of nodes to maintain strongly consistent cluster metadata. Store configuration, node state, deployments, and service information in etcd. Use watches to notify API servers of configuration changes, leases for temporary ownership, distributed locks for critical coordination, and automatic leader election for high availability. Monitor leader changes, lease expirations, watch latency, and cluster health to ensure reliable operation.

# Revision Notes

- ZooKeeper and etcd are coordination systems, not business databases.
- ZooKeeper stores metadata in hierarchical ZNodes, while etcd stores key-value pairs.
- Ephemeral nodes and leases automatically clean up state after failures.
- Distributed locks ensure exclusive ownership of shared resources.
- Watches provide event-driven notifications without polling.
- Service discovery and configuration management are core coordination responsibilities.
- Kubernetes relies on etcd, while legacy Kafka and Hadoop ecosystems relied heavily on ZooKeeper.
