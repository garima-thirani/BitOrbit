# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

What is the difference between High Availability and Disaster Recovery?

### Ideal Answer

High Availability minimizes service interruptions during component failures through redundancy and automatic failover. Disaster Recovery focuses on restoring services after catastrophic failures such as regional outages, cyberattacks, or large-scale data corruption.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why is replication not a substitute for backups?

### Ideal Answer

Replication copies both valid data and accidental changes, including deletions or corruption. Backups preserve historical versions, allowing recovery from logical errors that replication would immediately propagate.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Explain the 3-2-1 backup rule.

### Ideal Answer

Maintain three copies of the data, store them on at least two different types of storage media, and keep one copy offsite. This strategy protects against hardware failures, site disasters, and data corruption.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What are RPO and RTO?

### Ideal Answer

Recovery Point Objective (RPO) defines the maximum acceptable amount of data loss, while Recovery Time Objective (RTO) defines the maximum acceptable downtime before services must be restored.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the disaster recovery strategy for a global online banking platform.

### Ideal Answer

Deploy applications across multiple regions with Active-Passive or Active-Active architecture depending on business requirements. Use synchronous replication for critical financial data where appropriate, continuous transaction log archiving for point-in-time recovery, automated health checks for failover, offsite encrypted backups following the 3-2-1 rule, and regular disaster recovery exercises. Define strict RPO and RTO objectives, continuously monitor backup integrity, and validate recovery procedures through periodic restoration tests and chaos engineering experiments.

# Revision Notes

- High Availability minimizes downtime during routine failures.
- Disaster Recovery restores systems after catastrophic events.
- Replication improves availability but does not replace backups.
- Full, incremental, and differential backups serve different operational needs.
- Point-in-Time Recovery restores data to an exact moment before failure.
- RPO measures acceptable data loss; RTO measures acceptable downtime.
- Regular disaster recovery testing is essential to verify recovery plans.
- Chaos Engineering builds confidence that systems can survive unexpected failures.
