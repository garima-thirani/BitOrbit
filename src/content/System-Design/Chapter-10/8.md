# Engineering Notes

## Lock as Little as Possible

The longer

a distributed lock

is held,

the lower

the system's throughput.

Critical sections

should be

kept

as short

as possible.

Perform

only

the essential work

while

holding the lock.

---

## Coordination Has a Cost

Every coordination operation

requires

network communication.

Excessive coordination

reduces

throughput

and

increases latency.

Design systems

to minimize

shared mutable state

whenever possible.

# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why can't local mutexes coordinate distributed systems?

### Ideal Answer

Local mutexes synchronize threads within a single process or machine. Distributed systems consist of multiple machines that communicate over a network, requiring distributed coordination services to manage shared resources across servers.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the difference between optimistic and pessimistic locking?

### Ideal Answer

Optimistic locking assumes conflicts are rare and detects them using version checks or compare-and-swap operations. Pessimistic locking assumes conflicts are common and acquires locks before updates, preventing concurrent modifications but reducing concurrency.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why do distributed locks use leases?

### Ideal Answer

Leases ensure that locks automatically expire if the owning process crashes or becomes unreachable. This prevents permanent deadlocks caused by abandoned locks.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What role does etcd play in Kubernetes?

### Ideal Answer

etcd stores Kubernetes cluster state, including Pods, Deployments, Services, Nodes, ConfigMaps, and leader election information. It provides strongly consistent storage using the Raft consensus algorithm.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design a distributed scheduler where only one scheduler instance executes jobs at any given time.

### Ideal Answer

Use a Raft-based coordination service such as etcd. Each scheduler participates in leader election. The elected leader acquires a lease-based distributed lock and processes scheduled jobs. Followers remain idle while monitoring leader heartbeats. If the leader fails, the lease expires, a new leader is elected, and job execution resumes without duplicate processing.

# Revision Notes

- Distributed coordination synchronizes work across multiple machines.
- Distributed locks ensure exclusive access to shared resources.
- Lease-based locks recover automatically after failures.
- CAS enables lock-free optimistic concurrency.
- ZooKeeper and etcd provide distributed coordination primitives.
- etcd powers Kubernetes through strongly consistent cluster state.
- Coordination should be minimized because it increases latency.
- Keep critical sections short to maximize throughput.
