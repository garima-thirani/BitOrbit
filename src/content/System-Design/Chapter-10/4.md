# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why is replication used in distributed systems?

### Ideal Answer

Replication improves high availability, fault tolerance, disaster recovery, and read scalability by maintaining multiple copies of data across different servers or regions. It also enables faster access for geographically distributed users.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is the difference between synchronous and asynchronous replication?

### Ideal Answer

Synchronous replication waits for replicas to acknowledge a write before returning success, providing stronger consistency at the cost of higher latency. Asynchronous replication returns success immediately after the leader commits, offering better performance but allowing temporary replica lag and possible data loss during failures.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Explain Leader-Follower replication.

### Ideal Answer

A single leader accepts all write operations and propagates them to follower replicas. Reads can often be served from followers, improving read scalability while keeping write coordination simple.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What is Split Brain and why is it dangerous?

### Ideal Answer

Split Brain occurs when multiple nodes incorrectly believe they are the leader due to a network partition. Both may accept writes independently, causing conflicting updates and data divergence. Consensus algorithms and quorum mechanisms help prevent this situation.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the replication strategy for a global e-commerce platform.

### Ideal Answer

Use Leader-Follower replication for transactional databases, with synchronous or semi-synchronous replication for payments and inventory to minimize data loss. Deploy read replicas for product catalog and search workloads, replicate data across multiple regions for disaster recovery, and monitor replica lag continuously. For globally distributed user data requiring local writes, consider multi-leader replication with well-defined conflict resolution strategies.

# Revision Notes

- Replication creates multiple copies of data for availability and fault tolerance.
- Leader-Follower is the most common replication topology.
- Read replicas improve read scalability but introduce replica lag.
- Synchronous replication favors consistency; asynchronous replication favors performance.
- Multi-Leader supports regional writes but introduces conflict resolution challenges.
- Leaderless replication removes the leader bottleneck and relies on quorum protocols.
- Replication does not replace backups.
- Monitor replica lag, test failover, and design for split-brain prevention.
