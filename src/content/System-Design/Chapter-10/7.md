# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

Why do distributed systems need consensus?

### Ideal Answer

Distributed systems experience network partitions, server crashes, and delayed messages. Consensus ensures that all healthy nodes agree on critical decisions such as leader election, transaction ordering, and cluster membership despite these failures.

---

## Question 2

### Question

What is a quorum?

### Ideal Answer

A quorum is the minimum majority of nodes required to make a decision. For a cluster of N nodes, quorum is ⌊N/2⌋ + 1. Majority voting prevents multiple leaders from being elected simultaneously.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Explain the Raft algorithm.

### Ideal Answer

Raft is a distributed consensus algorithm that uses leader election and replicated logs to maintain a consistent state across nodes. Nodes operate as Followers, Candidates, or Leaders. When a leader fails, followers elect a new leader through majority voting, and all committed log entries remain consistent.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why is Raft preferred over Paxos in many production systems?

### Ideal Answer

Both algorithms provide strong consensus guarantees, but Raft was explicitly designed to be easier to understand, implement, and debug. This simplicity has led to widespread adoption in systems such as etcd, CockroachDB, and Kubernetes.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the leader election mechanism for a distributed database.

### Ideal Answer

Deploy an odd number of nodes, such as five, to enable majority voting. Use Raft for consensus. Leaders send periodic heartbeats, and followers start an election if heartbeats stop. A candidate becomes leader only after receiving votes from a quorum. All writes are accepted only by the leader and replicated to followers before being committed. This prevents split brain while enabling automatic failover.
