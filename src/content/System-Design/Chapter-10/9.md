# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

What is the difference between a Region and an Availability Zone?

### Ideal Answer

A Region is a geographically separate deployment location containing multiple Availability Zones. An Availability Zone is an isolated datacenter within a Region, designed to tolerate hardware failures but not full regional outages.

---

## Question 2

### Question

When should you use Active-Active instead of Active-Passive?

### Ideal Answer

Use Active-Active when users are globally distributed and low latency is critical. Multiple regions simultaneously serve traffic, improving availability and reducing latency. Active-Passive is simpler and better suited when disaster recovery is the primary goal.

---

## Question 3

### Question

Why is asynchronous geo-replication commonly used?

### Ideal Answer

Synchronous replication across continents introduces significant latency because writes must wait for remote acknowledgements. Asynchronous replication allows local writes to complete quickly while replicas update later, providing better user experience with eventual consistency.

---

## Question 4

### Question

What are RPO and RTO?

### Ideal Answer

RPO (Recovery Point Objective) defines the maximum acceptable amount of data loss after a disaster. RTO (Recovery Time Objective) defines the maximum acceptable downtime before services must be restored.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the global deployment strategy for a streaming platform serving users across North America, Europe, Asia, and Australia.

### Ideal Answer

Deploy independent regional clusters in each geography with Active-Active architecture. Use a global load balancer with health checks and geo-routing to direct users to the nearest healthy region. Store static assets on a CDN, asynchronously replicate user metadata across regions, keep user sessions in distributed storage, and maintain automated cross-region failover with well-defined RPO and RTO objectives. Localize data where required by regional regulations and minimize cross-region communication to reduce latency and costs.

# Revision Notes

- Multi-region deployments improve availability, disaster recovery, and latency.
- Availability Zones protect against datacenter failures, while Regions protect against regional failures.
- Active-Passive simplifies disaster recovery; Active-Active maximizes availability.
- Geo-replication keeps data synchronized across regions.
- Global load balancers route users to the nearest healthy region.
- CDNs reduce latency for static content.
- Data locality improves performance and supports regulatory compliance.
- RPO and RTO define disaster recovery objectives.
