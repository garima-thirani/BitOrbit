# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

What is the difference between partitioning and sharding?

### Ideal Answer

Partitioning is the general technique of dividing data into smaller pieces. Sharding is a specific form of horizontal partitioning where partitions are distributed across multiple database servers, enabling both storage and workload scaling.

---

## Question 2

### Question

When would you choose hash-based sharding over range-based sharding?

### Ideal Answer

Hash-based sharding is preferred when workloads require even data distribution and avoiding hotspots. Range-based sharding is more suitable when range queries are common, although it risks uneven load if new records concentrate within a single range.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What makes a good shard key?

### Ideal Answer

A good shard key has high cardinality, distributes data evenly, remains relatively stable, and aligns with common query patterns to minimize cross-shard operations.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why are cross-shard queries expensive?

### Ideal Answer

Cross-shard queries require contacting multiple databases, executing partial queries independently, transferring results across the network, and merging them before returning a response. This increases latency and resource usage compared to single-shard queries.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the sharding strategy for a global e-commerce platform.

### Ideal Answer

Shard customer data by Customer ID using hash-based sharding to achieve even distribution. Use functional partitioning to separate Orders, Payments, Inventory, and Search into independent databases. Geo-shard data across regions to reduce latency. Implement shard-aware routing, monitor hotspot metrics continuously, support online rebalancing, and minimize cross-shard transactions by colocating related data whenever possible.

# Revision Notes

- Partitioning divides data; sharding distributes partitions across multiple servers.
- Horizontal partitioning splits rows, while vertical partitioning splits columns.
- Functional partitioning aligns with business capabilities.
- Hash-based sharding balances load, whereas range-based sharding supports efficient range queries.
- Choosing the correct shard key is critical to long-term scalability.
- Hotspots reduce scalability and must be monitored continuously.
- Rebalancing and resharding are unavoidable as systems grow.
- Minimize cross-shard operations through thoughtful data modeling.
