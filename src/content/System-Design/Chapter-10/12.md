# Case Study 1 – Google Search

---

## Business Requirements

Google Search must

- Search trillions of webpages
- Handle billions of searches every day
- Return results in milliseconds
- Remain available worldwide
- Continuously crawl the internet

---

## Challenges

```text
Massive Data

↓

Low Latency

↓

High Availability

↓

Constant Updates

↓

Global Scale
```

---

## High-Level Architecture

```text
Users

↓

Global DNS

↓

Nearest Google Edge

↓

Load Balancer

↓

Search Frontend

↓

Index Servers

↓

Ranking Service

↓

Result Aggregation

↓

User
```

---

## Data Storage

Google stores

massive inverted indexes.

Instead of

```text
Document

↓

Words
```

Search uses

```text
Word

↓

Documents
```

This enables

extremely fast lookup.

---

## Scaling Strategy

Google scales by

- Thousands of servers
- Sharding search indexes
- Replication
- Regional deployments
- Edge routing

---

## Replication

Indexes

exist

in multiple datacenters.

```text
US

↓

Europe

↓

Asia
```

If one region fails,

another serves requests.

---

## Consistency

Search indexes

are

eventually consistent.

New webpages

may take

seconds

or minutes

to appear.

Acceptable

for search.

---

## Lessons

✔ Massive horizontal scaling

✔ Read-heavy optimization

✔ Aggressive caching

✔ Distributed indexing

---

## Interview Takeaway

Search engines

favor

availability,

replication,

partitioning,

and caching

over

strong consistency.

# Case Study 2 – Amazon DynamoDB

---

## Business Requirements

Provide

- Low latency
- High availability
- Automatic scaling
- Global distribution
- No operational management

---

## Architecture

```text
Clients

↓

API

↓

Partition Router

↓

Storage Nodes

↓

Replication
```

---

## Partitioning

Uses

consistent hashing.

Benefits

- Automatic scaling
- Minimal data movement
- Easy node addition

---

## Replication

Every partition

stores

multiple replicas.

Typical

Replication Factor

```
3
```

---

## Consistency

Developers choose

Strong Reads

or

Eventually Consistent Reads.

---

## Failure Recovery

Automatic

node replacement.

Automatic

rebalancing.

Automatic

replication.

---

## Lessons

✔ Leaderless architecture

✔ Horizontal scaling

✔ Tunable consistency

✔ Self-healing cluster

---

## Interview Takeaway

DynamoDB

optimizes

availability,

partition tolerance,

and elasticity.

# Case Study 3 – Netflix

---

## Business Requirements

- Millions of concurrent streams
- Global availability
- Low startup latency
- Zero downtime deployments

---

## Architecture

```text
Users

↓

Open Connect CDN

↓

Regional APIs

↓

Microservices

↓

Distributed Storage
```

---

## Video Delivery

Videos

are

cached

inside

Open Connect

near ISPs.

Most traffic

never reaches

Netflix origin.

---

## Scaling Strategy

Each microservice

scales independently.

Examples

- Playback
- Recommendations
- Billing
- Search

---

## Disaster Recovery

Netflix assumes

servers fail constantly.

Uses

Chaos Engineering

to validate resilience.

---

## Lessons

✔ CDN first

✔ Independent scaling

✔ Active-Active regions

✔ Failure testing

---

## Interview Takeaway

Netflix demonstrates

how CDNs,

microservices,

and regional architectures

combine

for planet-scale streaming.

# Case Study 4 – Meta (Facebook TAO)

---

## Business Requirements

Store

social graph

for

billions of users.

Operations include

- Friend lists
- Followers
- Likes
- Comments
- News Feed

---

## Challenge

One database

cannot

store

or serve

the entire graph.

---

## Architecture

```text
Users

↓

Regional Frontend

↓

TAO Cache

↓

MySQL Storage

↓

Replication
```

---

## Why TAO?

Traditional databases

became

the bottleneck.

TAO

acts

as

a distributed

graph cache.

---

## Scaling Strategy

- Regional caching
- Massive replication
- Graph partitioning
- Read optimization

---

## Consistency

Eventually consistent.

Perfect freshness

is

less important

than

fast responses.

---

## Lessons

✔ Specialized storage

✔ Distributed caching

✔ Graph optimization

---

## Interview Takeaway

General-purpose databases

are often replaced

with

domain-specific systems

at internet scale.

---

## Business Requirements

- Protect websites
- Deliver content
- Block attacks
- Execute edge code
- Low latency worldwide

---

## Architecture

```text
Users

↓

Nearest Edge

↓

Firewall

↓

Cache

↓

Origin
```

---

## Anycast

Cloudflare

advertises

one IP

globally.

Internet routing

sends users

to

the nearest

edge location.

---

## Edge Computing

Cloudflare Workers

execute

JavaScript

near users.

Many requests

never reach

the origin.

---

## Scaling Strategy

- Hundreds of edge locations
- Distributed cache
- Anycast routing
- Edge execution

---

## Lessons

✔ Network-first architecture

✔ Edge computing

✔ Massive caching

closer

to users

instead of

moving users

to computation.

# Final Engineering Lessons

1. Scale horizontally before vertically.
2. Replication improves availability, not write scalability.
3. Sharding is inevitable at internet scale.
4. Consensus prevents split-brain and inconsistent state.
5. Cache aggressively, but invalidate carefully.
6. Design for regional failures from day one.
7. Automate deployment, recovery, and monitoring.
8. Optimize for business requirements, not theoretical perfection.
9. Measure everything before optimizing.
10. Every architectural decision is a trade-off.
