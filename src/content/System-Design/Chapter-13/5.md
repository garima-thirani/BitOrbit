# Interview Questions & Solutions

## Question 1

### Question

When should you use a Hash Index instead of a B+ Tree?

### Ideal Answer

Hash indexes are ideal for exact equality lookups because they provide near O(1) average lookup time. They should not be used for range queries, sorting, or ordered traversal because hash values have no inherent ordering.

---

## Question 2

### Question

Why are Bitmap indexes suitable for data warehouses?

### Ideal Answer

Bitmap indexes are highly compressed and support efficient bitwise operations such as AND and OR. They perform exceptionally well on read-heavy analytical workloads with low-cardinality columns but are inefficient for frequent updates.

---

## Question 3

### Question

What is an Inverted Index?

### Ideal Answer

An inverted index maps words to the documents containing them, enabling fast keyword searches. It is the foundation of modern search engines such as Elasticsearch and Google Search.

---

## Question 4

### Question

Why can't B+ Trees efficiently support full-text search?

### Ideal Answer

B+ Trees organize data by sorted keys, making them efficient for exact and range queries. Full-text search requires tokenization, word positions, stemming, ranking, and inverted mappings, which B+ Trees are not designed to support.

---

## Question 5 (FAANG)

### Question

Design the indexing strategy for a global e-commerce platform supporting product lookup and keyword search.

### Ideal Answer

Use B+ Tree indexes for ProductID, CategoryID, and Price to support exact lookups and range queries. Use composite indexes for frequently filtered attributes such as (CategoryID, BrandID). Integrate a search engine such as Elasticsearch with inverted indexes for keyword search across product titles and descriptions. Use bitmap indexes in analytical warehouses for low-cardinality dimensions such as ProductStatus, and monitor query patterns to refine index design.

# Revision Notes

- B+ Trees are best for exact lookups, range scans, sorting, and ordered traversal.
- Hash indexes provide extremely fast equality lookups but cannot support range queries.
- Bitmap indexes excel in analytical workloads with low-cardinality columns.
- Full-text indexes use inverted indexes to map words to documents.
- Prefix indexes reduce storage by indexing only the beginning of values.
- Trigram indexes accelerate LIKE and fuzzy-search operations.
- Large-scale systems often combine multiple index types to optimize different query patterns.
